# Atmospheric Dryness in Southeastern Türkiye (1990–2025)

## Overview

This study investigates atmospheric dryness and evaporative demand in Southeastern Türkiye during 1990–2025.

The study was conducted because drought assessment based only on precipitation does not fully represent atmospheric water demand. Temperature, humidity, radiation, and wind also influence atmospheric dryness and evapotranspiration.

The analysis therefore focuses on **Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)** and **Potential Evapotranspiration (PET)** together with major climatic variables.

---

## Objectives

- Assess long-term climatic variability.
- Examine atmospheric dryness.
- Evaluate VPD and PET.
- Identify temporal trends and potential change points.
- Discuss environmental implications for agriculture and water resources.

---

## Data

**Data Source:** NASA POWER  
**Study Period:** 1990–2025

Variables:

- Temperature
- Maximum and minimum temperature
- Relative humidity
- Precipitation
- Wind speed
- Solar radiation

---

## Methods

The study uses:

- Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)
- Potential Evapotranspiration (PET)
- Mann–Kendall trend test
- Sen's slope estimator
- Pettitt change-point test
- Seasonal and correlation analysis

# Main Findings

The analysis indicates that atmospheric dryness is a **multi-dimensional hydroclimatic phenomenon**.

The main findings are:

- Atmospheric dryness varies considerably over time.
- VPD provides important information about atmospheric moisture demand.
- PET provides an integrated measure of evaporative demand.
- Temperature and humidity are important controls on atmospheric dryness.
- Trend and change-point analyses provide complementary information about temporal variability.

---

# Environmental Significance

Changes in atmospheric dryness and evaporative demand may affect:

- Agricultural irrigation requirements
- Vegetation water stress
- Soil-water availability
- Groundwater demand
- Regional water-resource management

These results highlight the importance of considering atmospheric water demand together with precipitation when assessing drought and climate-change impacts.

---

# Conclusion

This study provides an integrated assessment of atmospheric dryness and evaporative demand in Southeastern Türkiye during 1990–2025.

The combination of **VPD, PET, climatic variables, and statistical trend analysis** provides a broader perspective on hydroclimatic variability than precipitation-based assessment alone.

The findings provide a useful basis for future research on drought, climate change, agricultural water demand, and regional water-resource management.
