# Atmospheric Dryness in Southeastern Türkiye (1990–2025)

## Overview

This study investigates long-term atmospheric dryness and evaporative demand in Southeastern Türkiye during the 1990–2025 period.

The study was conducted because conventional drought assessments often focus mainly on precipitation, while atmospheric water demand is also strongly influenced by temperature, humidity, radiation, and wind.

The research therefore evaluates atmospheric dryness using **Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)** and **Potential Evapotranspiration (PET)** together with major climatic variables.

---

## Objectives

The main objectives are to:

- Assess long-term climatic variability.
- Examine atmospheric dryness.
- Evaluate Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD).
- Assess Potential Evapotranspiration (PET).
- Identify temporal trends.
- Investigate potential change points.
- Evaluate the environmental significance of atmospheric water demand.

---

## Data

**Data source:** NASA POWER

**Study period:** 1990–2025

The analysis includes:

- Air temperature
- Maximum temperature
- Minimum temperature
- Relative humidity
- Precipitation
- Wind speed
- Solar radiation

---

## Methods

The study applies:

- Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)
- Potential Evapotranspiration (PET)
- Mann–Kendall trend test
- Sen's slope estimator
- Pettitt change-point test
- Seasonal analysis
- Correlation analysis

These methods provide a combined assessment of atmospheric moisture conditions, evaporative demand, and temporal variability.

# Main Findings

The analysis demonstrates that atmospheric dryness is a multi-dimensional hydroclimatic phenomenon.

Temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, radiation, and wind collectively influence atmospheric water demand.

VPD provides information about atmospheric moisture deficit, while PET represents potential evaporative demand.

The combined assessment of these indicators provides a broader perspective on hydroclimatic variability than precipitation-based analysis alone.

---

# Environmental Significance

Changes in atmospheric dryness and evaporative demand may have implications for:

- Agricultural irrigation requirements
- Crop and vegetation water stress
- Soil-water availability
- Groundwater demand
- Regional water-resource management
- Drought vulnerability

These findings highlight the importance of considering atmospheric water demand in regional climate and drought assessments.

---

# Conclusion

This study provides an integrated assessment of atmospheric dryness and evaporative demand in Southeastern Türkiye during 1990–2025.

The combination of VPD, PET, climatic variables, trend analysis, and change-point detection provides a comprehensive framework for investigating atmospheric hydroclimatic variability.

The results emphasize that atmospheric dryness should be evaluated together with precipitation and other climatic controls when assessing drought risk, environmental vulnerability, and future water-resource challenges.
